- ==HMM==: A framework developed by David Bianco and Sqrrl to define the maturity of threat hunting practices within an organization.
- ==Hunting Maturity Levels (HM)==: Five levels of maturity ranging from HM0 to HM4, with increasing sophistication and proactivity as levels rise.
- ==Automation’s Role==: Increases with maturity levels, shifting from manual to automated threat hunting processes.

![1](/Course-Notes/.assets/Pasted_image_20241129135751.png)
HM0:
- The organization relies on alerting - ==Reactive==.
- ==Reviews Alerts== Generated by tools such as IDS.
- ==Depends on Detection== and alerting to become aware of threats within its network.
- An HM0 is ==limited to IDS== informants.
- Not a hunt operation, but the ==review stage==.

HM1:
- Organizations still rely on an IDS for alerts.
- ==Collect information== to look for new threats.
- Uses ==threat intelligence== feeds to direct them.
- Hunts through data collected from their IT systems.
- HM1 mainly perform ==manual hunt== operations.

HM2:
- Can source ==external hunt techniques==.
- Sift ==large amounts of information== from their IT systems.
- Allowed to identify ==multiple categories== of malicious activity.
- ==Active Hunting==, rather than reactive.

HM3:
- HM3 organizations are ==innovative==.
- They analyze ==multiple data types==.
- Uses analysis to ==identify new malicious activity==.
- ==Self reliant resources== and techniques when hunting.
- Department that ==publishes procedures==.
- ==Advanced techniques== such as data visualization or machine learning to help their analysts see past an individual alert to ==identify patterns== in multiple alerts.

HM4:
- Same capabilities as HM3, but better.
- Automate many tactical-level analysis procedures.
- ==Automated== threat hunting.
- ==Written scripts or programs== based on intelligence and hunt procedures.
- Focused on ==creating new hunt methods== instead of implementing existing ones.
