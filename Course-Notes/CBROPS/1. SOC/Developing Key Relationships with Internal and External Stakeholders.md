Facts to Memorize

- Internal stakeholders include executive management, incident response team manager, incident response technical lead, human resources, IT, governance, risk and compliance (GRC) group, legal counsel, public relations, and helpdesk.
- External stakeholders include cloud providers, other response teams, media, law enforcement, incident reporters, and software vendors.
- The organization must have a predefined communication plan for both internal and external stakeholders during a cybersecurity incident.

Reference Information

- Incident response communication plan: A structured approach to communicate during an incident.
- Point of contact (POC): A designated individual responsible for communication with external stakeholders.
- Cybersecurity incident response team (CSIRT): A team that handles cybersecurity incidents and coordinates with internal and external stakeholders.

# Internal Stakeholders in Incident Response

### Overview of Internal Stakeholders

- Internal stakeholders are personnel from various departments within an organization who assist the Security Operations Center (SOC) during incident response efforts.
- Their expertise is crucial for effective incident management, as they provide guidance and support based on their specific roles.
- Identifying these stakeholders is essential for a coordinated response to cybersecurity incidents.
- The SOC must understand the responsibilities and interactions of these stakeholders to ensure a smooth incident response process.
- Clear rules of engagement must be established to define how the SOC will seek assistance from these stakeholders.
- An information escalation process should be defined to facilitate communication during incidents.

### Roles and Responsibilities of Key Internal Stakeholders

- ****Executive Management Team****: Responsible for coordinating incident response, establishing policies, and facilitating communication across departments.
- ****Incident Response Team Manager****: Ensures proper execution of incident response activities and acts as a liaison between the SOC and other teams.
- ****Incident Response Technical Lead****: Verifies the technical accuracy of incident response activities and collaborates with the SOC to ensure technical integrity.
- ****Human Resources****: Manages personal identity information and coordinates security compliance and education on cybersecurity awareness.
- ****IT (Network Security Engineer)****: Manages network infrastructure and collaborates with the SOC to enhance the organization's security posture.
- ****Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) Group****: Aligns technology objectives with risk management and regulatory compliance.

### Interaction Between SOC and Internal Stakeholders

- Effective communication is vital for incident response; the SOC must maintain open lines with all internal stakeholders.
- Regular training and updates on cybersecurity policies can enhance the readiness of internal stakeholders during incidents.
- The SOC should establish a communication plan that outlines how and when to engage with each internal stakeholder during an incident.
- Collaboration with Human Resources is critical for managing incidents involving internal bad actors or data breaches.
- The SOC must ensure that all stakeholders understand their roles and responsibilities in the incident response process.
- Post-incident reviews should involve all internal stakeholders to assess the response and improve future efforts.

# External Stakeholders in Incident Response

### Overview of External Stakeholders

- External stakeholders are entities outside the organization that may need to be informed or involved during a cybersecurity incident.
- Building relationships with these stakeholders is crucial for effective incident management and communication.
- Organizations must predetermine the information to share with each external stakeholder before an incident occurs.
- The engagement with external stakeholders often happens quickly and unexpectedly, necessitating pre-established communication protocols.
- A point of contact (POC) should be assigned to manage interactions with external stakeholders during incidents.
- Legal consultation is essential before sharing information with external stakeholders to ensure compliance with laws and regulations.

### Roles and Responsibilities of Key External Stakeholders

- ****Cloud Providers****: Responsible for providing cloud services and collaborating with the SOC to mitigate data loss.
- ****Other Response Teams****: Share best practices for managing breaches and collaborate on incident response efforts.
- ****Media****: Informed about security breaches as dictated by internal public affairs and legal departments to manage public perception.
- ****Law Enforcement****: Engaged for disciplinary actions and provided with breach details as required by law.
- ****Incident Reporter****: Analyzes cyber threats and receives breach details as mandated by government agencies.
- ****Software Vendors****: Provide information on software vulnerabilities that may affect the organization during a breach.

### Interaction Between SOC and External Stakeholders

- The SOC must establish clear communication channels with external stakeholders to ensure timely and accurate information sharing during incidents.
- Regular training and updates on incident response protocols can help external stakeholders understand their roles.
- The SOC should have predefined templates for communicating with external stakeholders to streamline the process during an incident.
- Collaboration with law enforcement may involve legal considerations, requiring the SOC to work closely with the legal department.
- Engaging with media requires careful planning to ensure that the organization's message is consistent and compliant with legal guidelines.
- Post-incident evaluations should include feedback from external stakeholders to improve future interactions.
