>Each organization that implements a CSIRT should understand the inherent structure and needs of the environment in which the CSIRT will operate.

A CSIRT can be described in terms of 
- ==What it sets out to do== 
	- **Mission statement:** CSIRT high-level goals, objectives, and priorities.
- Typical CSIRT mission statements include the following:
	- Responsible for global 24-hour monitoring, investigation, and response to cybersecurity incidents.
	- Engage in proactive threat assessment, mitigation planning, incident detection and response, incident trending with analysis, and the development of security architecture.
- ==For whom==
	- **Constituency:** Define the specific community that the CSIRT was established to serve, and the CSIRT relationship to the constituency.
- ==What its roots look like== 
	- **Organizational structure:** In most corporate environments, a CSIRT must be well positioned in the business structure of the organization and commonly resides within, or has some overlap, with the organization SOC.
- ==And who its peers are== 
	- **Relationships:** The CSIRT's relationship to other teams. For example, the U.S. Army, Air Force, and Navy CSIRTs serve their own constituencies, while the U.S. Department of Defense Computer Emergency Response Team coordinates across all the U.S. military teams.
